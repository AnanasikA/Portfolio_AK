Your customers need a place to learn more about your business, but does that always mean you need a full website?

Not necessarily.

In some cases, a **landing page** can be a much better solution — especially when you want to promote one specific service, product or campaign.

**Landing page vs website — which one should you choose?** The answer depends on what you want your website to achieve.

## What Is a Landing Page?

A landing page is a single web page designed around one specific goal.

That goal could be:

- submitting a contact form
- booking a consultation
- purchasing a product
- registering for an event
- downloading a resource
- contacting your business

Instead of navigating through multiple pages, visitors receive the most important information in one place.

## What Does a Landing Page Include?

A typical landing page may include:

- a clear headline and offer
- key benefits
- service or product description
- photos or portfolio examples
- customer testimonials
- frequently asked questions
- contact form
- a clear call to action

Every section should guide the visitor towards one specific action.

## How Is a Business Website Different?

A traditional business website is usually more extensive.

Instead of one page, it may contain several different pages, such as:

- Home
- About
- Services
- Portfolio
- Blog
- Contact

This gives you more space to explain what your company does, present different services and build trust with potential customers.

## When Should You Choose a Landing Page?

A landing page is particularly useful when you are promoting **one specific offer or service**.

For example:

- a Google Ads campaign
- a new service
- a training course
- an event
- a special promotion
- a single product
- a lead generation campaign

When someone clicks on an advertisement, they can immediately see information related to the offer they were interested in.

They don't need to browse through several pages to find it.

## When Is a Business Website Better?

A full website is usually the better choice if you want to build a **long-term online presence for your business**.

Consider a full website if:

- you offer several services
- you want to showcase your portfolio
- you plan to publish blog articles
- SEO is important to your business
- you need to provide detailed information
- customers need to learn more before contacting you

A business website also gives you much more flexibility for future growth.

## Landing Page vs Website for SEO

A landing page can appear in Google search results, but its SEO opportunities are usually more limited than those of a larger website.

A full website allows you to create separate pages targeting different services and search queries.

For example:

- `/web-design`
- `/ecommerce-development`
- `/landing-pages`
- `/website-redesign`
- `/blog`

Each page can target a different topic and answer different questions potential customers are searching for.

If **organic search is an important source of customers for your business**, a full website usually provides more opportunities for long-term SEO growth.

## Landing Pages for Advertising

This is where landing pages can be especially effective.

A dedicated landing page works very well with paid advertising campaigns.

Imagine an advertisement saying:

**"Professional Business Website – Get a Free Quote"**

After clicking the ad, the visitor lands directly on a page focused entirely on that offer.

There are fewer distractions and no need to search through the website.

The entire page can focus on one goal — for example, getting the visitor to submit an enquiry.

## Which Option Is Cheaper?

A landing page is usually less expensive than a complete business website.

The reason is simple: there are fewer pages to design and generally less content to prepare.

However, that doesn't mean a landing page should be created quickly without proper planning.

An effective landing page still needs:

- a clear structure
- professional design
- mobile responsiveness
- fast loading times
- effective content
- a strong call to action

Its main purpose is to **turn visitors into potential customers**.

## Can You Have Both a Website and Landing Pages?

Yes — and in many cases, this is the best solution.

A business can have a main website and create additional landing pages for specific services or marketing campaigns.

For example:

**Business website**

Presents your company, services, portfolio and other important information.

**Landing page**

Promotes one specific service or campaign and focuses on generating enquiries.

You don't always have to choose between the two.

## What Should a New Business Choose?

If you're just starting a business and offer one specific service, a professionally designed landing page may be enough at the beginning.

It allows you to establish an online presence without immediately investing in a larger website.

However, if your business offers several services and you want to build visibility in Google over time, a complete business website is usually the better option.

The most important question isn't:

**"Which one is better?"**

It's:

**"What do I want my website to achieve?"**

## Final Thoughts

A **landing page** is a great choice when you have one specific offer and want to generate leads or run an advertising campaign.

A **business website** is usually better when you want to present your entire company, improve your SEO and build a long-term online presence.

For many businesses, the best solution is actually to use both.
